What this compares

Three in-tree decimators, each at all three merged-mass compensation modes, plus wl's own simplify as an external reference point. Every cell reduces the same scene to the same splat count at the same LOD, so the only variables are which splats survive and what happens to the mass of the ones that don't.

  • none — merged mass above unit alpha is discarded. Shipping default.
  • alpha — the excess is kept as over-unity peak opacity, rendered as a saturated plateau.
  • scale — the footprint is inflated until the mass fits under unit alpha.

The numbers behind the images

Mean rank is the column to read: PSNR and gap have very different dynamic ranges per scene, so averaging raw values lets one scene dominate. Rank is 1 = best of 10. gap is the percentage of pixels more than 20 luma off the reference, split into darker (holes) and brighter (over-bright). PSNR and gap disagree by design — a diffuse haze costs a lot of MSE but is barely visible, while a few hard black holes cost little MSE and are the first thing you notice. Where they disagree, the images decide.

Findings

1. Both new decimators clearly beat the shipped uniform one

Compared like-for-like at the shipping none policy, mean gap rank is adaptive 5.0 and voxel 5.8 against uniform's 8.2 — uniform is last of ten. (Across all three policies the ranges overlap, because compensation outweighs the allocator: see finding 4.) The margin is not marginal — on bad-sky and bad-snow-2 the gap metric falls from 36.1% and 24.6% (uniform, no compensation) to 5.1% and 4.3% (adaptive) and 8.2% and 3.9% (voxel). Those are the two scenes where uniform allocation starves whole regions, and error-driven allocation is exactly the fix. PSNR agrees: +10.6 dB on bad-sky, +9.8 dB on bad-snow-2.

2. Build cost is not a differentiator

decimatorfull LOD pyramid

uniform 10m10s ×1.00 ×0.366
adaptive 22m10s ×2.18 ×0.455
voxel 12m21s ×1.21 ×0.425
wl 17m47s ×1.75 ×0.423

Uniform and adaptive cascade, so every level is a by-product of one run; voxel and wl are single-pass and pay a full source read per level. Compensation is time-neutral (within 2%). Fragment counts are a GPU-measured render-cost proxy — alpha is the cheapest of the three modes (×0.16–×0.38) because brighter splats terminate rays sooner.

Voxel costs 21% more than uniform to build a full pyramid and adaptive 118% more, but all four are the same order of magnitude and none of this should decide the outcome. One note worth keeping: voxel's per-level cost is nearly flat (46s, 42s, 37s, 40s, 46s on bad-spots while the target fell 16×) because the source read dominates. Since its allocator already produces a full ordered pick sequence, one pass could in principle emit every level and collapse its pyramid cost to roughly a third of uniform's — untested, but the largest cost lever available.

3. The exception that matters: bad-snow

Among none variants, bad-snow is the one scene where the shipped uniform decimator wins — gap 37.9% versus voxel's 49.5%, an 11.6-point regression, and uniform also takes the best none PSNR there (19.26). It is the most aggressive reduction in the set, 1.56% of splats kept. This is not a cascade artifact: voxel is single-shot here, so the deficit is the allocator's own behaviour at that ratio.

Compensation closes it, and then some. uniform / alpha reaches 35.05% — better than the shipped uniform / none at 37.93% and the best result on this scene anywhere in the table. voxel / alpha gets to 44.13% and adaptive / alpha to 38.95%. So bad-snow is not an argument against voxel so much as an argument that this scene needs compensation: adopting voxel with none would ship the regression, adopting it with alpha would not.

4. alpha wins on every decimator — after two correctness fixes

An earlier version of this report measured alpha through two bugs and concluded it was worse than none. With both fixed, all three alpha variants beat wl on both metrics, and they take the top three places:

adaptive / alpha3.83.4
uniform / alpha4.64.2

adaptive / none5.25.0

Compensation now matters more than the choice of decimator. Every decimator's alpha variant outranks every decimator's none variant. That reframes the decision: the allocator question and the compensation question are separable, and compensation is the bigger lever.

It also changes the bad-snow story. uniform / alpha reaches 35.05 gap there — the best result on that scene in the whole table, beating the shipped uniform / none at 37.93. bad-snow is no longer a scene where compensation fails to help.

Bug 1 — input range. alphaDecode() applied the over-unity range to whatever it read, including stock 0..1 sources, inflating input mass ×3.8 at α=0.5 and ×6.6 at α=0.99 — non-uniformly, so it did not cancel in normalised merge weights. Before the fix: emitted mass 5.4–6.2× source, 20–77% of survivors pinned at the D=4 ceiling, and on bad-cloud 97.5% of splats rendering as saturated flat-topped discs, costing the sky region 34 dB. Fixed by splitting inputAlphaMax (the range the input is in) from alphaMax (the range we write).

Bug 2 — pass-through survivors. At a 2× step most output splats are not merged at all, they are block-copied from the input. Merged splats go through alphaEncode and land in the output convention; copied ones kept the input's convention while the file was declared to be in ours, so the untouched majority read ~4× too bright. This hit adaptive hardest (mass ×6.2, and its alpha cells scoring below its none cells). Fixed by converting opacity on the pass-through path in all three merge streams.

Verified after both fixes: mass conservation is ×1.000 for uniform, adaptive and voxel on bad-sky, bad-cloud and bad-snow-2, with ceiling saturation at 0.0–0.1%. bad-spots sits at ×0.85–0.90, which is genuine clamping at its 3.13% keep rate rather than a convention error. The bad-cloud sky recovered from 23.08 dB to 56.95 dB, matching none's 56.96.

4a. The other two compensation modes

  • none — the shipping default — is now beaten by alpha on every decimator, but it remains the only option that needs no format or renderer change, and it is still best-in-scene for adaptive on bad-sky (5.10 vs 5.62).
  • alpha is also always the cheapest to render (×0.16–×0.38 fragments), though per the caveat that is early ray termination, not a smaller footprint.
  • scale wins only bad-snow-2 (3.34%) and is consistently the most expensive to render (up to ×0.77). Weakest of the three, and unchanged by both fixes — it encodes compensation in geometry, so it never depended on the opacity convention.

5. wl's shipped default still has the best average rank

wl takes the best mean rank on both metrics (3.2 PSNR, 3.6 gap), and this is a like-for-like comparison against voxel — both reach the final count in a single pass. Head to head on gap, though, it is a split rather than a sweep: voxel wins bad-sky (8.2 vs 10.9) and bad-snow-2 (3.9 vs 17.7), wl wins bad-cloud (14.8 vs 19.2) and bad-snow (41.5 vs 49.5), and bad-spots ties at 20.85%. On PSNR voxel takes 3 of 5.

What earns wl its rank is consistency, not peak quality: it is never the best cell on any scene and never close to the worst. Voxel is better where it wins and much worse on bad-snow. Which of those profiles is preferable is a product question — a decimator that is never embarrassing may be worth more than one with a higher ceiling and one bad scene.

Note that wl's default also includes an opacity pre-filter (0.05) that drops faint gaussians before merging, and per-tile budgeting. Neither exists in our decimators, and both are cheap to try.

6. Why wl looks unlike every voxel column — it is allocation, not compensation

wl's default stacks three things on the clustering our voxel decimator ports from its write-up: an opacity pre-filter (0.05), a coverage boost (0.75, which inflates survivor scales and is the analogue of our scale mode), and per-KD-leaf budgeting (partition_limit=1e6; absent from the write-up — we allocate globally). The opacity filter is nearly irrelevant — worth under 1.2 points on every scene, even on bad-sky where it discards 16% of the input. An earlier assumption in this work was that the filter penalised the sky and cloud scenes; that is measurably false.

On bad-snow-2 neither knob explains anything. With both off, wl is still at 17.35 against voxel's 3.85. The difference is where the budget landed. On a 48³ grid, at identical splat counts, wl's occupied cells are almost a strict subset of voxel's:

The boost asymmetry, and why porting wl's tiling would be a mistake

The same nominal lever moves the two systems in opposite directions:

  • wl on bad-sky: 39.12 → 11.30 with the boost on. A 28-point rescue.
  • voxel on bad-sky: 8.22 → 10.05 with scale. Slightly worse.

wl's per-leaf allocation leaves holes that scale inflation papers over, so wl needs its boost. Our global allocation already covers, so inflation only adds blur. Adopting wl's tiling would therefore import the very problem its boost exists to fix — and the boost is the part we already have and mostly do not want.

This does not make wl worse everywhere: it still genuinely beats voxel on bad-cloud (14.83 vs 19.20) and bad-snow (41.52 vs 49.54), where coverage breadth is not the deciding factor. Whatever wl does better on those two is not explained by either knob or by tiling, and is not yet understood.

6a. The decimators' own error metric still assumes none

Separate from the output-format problem below, the cost that drives merge selection hardcodes the none policy. Both the CPU and GPU paths compute the merged opacity a candidate would get as min(1, W / area) (edge-cost-cpu.ts, gpu-edge-cost.ts), but the real ceiling under alpha is profileMass(alphaMax) — 6.63 at alphaMax 4 — and under scale the merge grows the footprint until the mass fits. Neither is modelled.

So every decimator ranks candidate merges as if the excess mass will be thrown away. Under compensation it will not be, which means merges that lose a lot of mass are penalised when they are in fact cheap. The selection is systematically conservative in exactly the cases compensation was added to rescue — and the alpha numbers above are therefore a lower bound: they were achieved with an allocator that never exploited the headroom it was given.

The fix is to use the active ceiling in the cost and mirror the scale inflation, reusing the same branch the merge takes. Two call sites, and the WGSL needs the ceiling baked in as the decode kernel already does. It is deliberately not applied here, because it changes selection and would invalidate this comparison; none is unaffected (its ceiling is 1), so the blast radius is the alpha and scale cells only.

7. alpha's real blocker: three places assume plain 0..1 opacity

Now that alpha measures best, what stops us shipping it is that the over-unity convention is implicit. Three consumers assume opacity is plain 0..1, and only one of them is the renderer:

consumernonescalealpha
renderercorrectcorrectneeds ST_ALPHA_MAX env var
LOD error pass (buildErrorCache)correctcorrectunder-reads alpha ~4×
SOG writercorrectcorrectsilently destroys it

The LOD error pass uses plain sigmoid() on stored opacity, so the field-L2 error — and any level assignment built on it — is wrong for alpha output. The SOG writer quantises sigmoid(opacity) into 8 bits over 0..1, so an over-unity D stored as logit(D/4) comes back as D/4 and is written as that: the compensation is not mis-rendered, it is permanently lost and the SOG is ~4× too transparent. scale is clean everywhere precisely because it encodes compensation in geometry, which every format already carries faithfully.

The fix that unblocks all three: store compensation per gaussian

Write one extra scalar per gaussian — the owed-mass ratio κ = W / ellipsoidArea(s) — and leave opacity and scale in the plain 0..1 convention. Every mode then becomes a reader policy:

  • none — ignore κ, clamp alpha at 1. Bit-identical to today, and what any third-party tool gets for free.
  • alpha — use κ as the shape parameter D.
  • scale — inflate scales by √κ. Exact, not iterative, since ellipsoidArea is homogeneous of degree 2.

Both non-trivial policies are render-time transforms, implementable in the shader. The consequences are larger than tidiness: one decimation run serves all three modes, so this whole comparison becomes a render-time toggle instead of 45 cascades; the D=4 ceiling and its 20–77% saturation disappear because κ is unbounded; selection becomes policy-independent, so the modes are finally compared on compensation alone rather than on differently-allocated splat sets; and the class of bug fixed above cannot recur, because no file's opacity range is ever implicit again. For compressed formats κ needs a quantised channel — log2(κ) in 8 bits over [0,8] covers the observed range — and a render-time scale policy changes the projected radius, so the binner must use the inflated value.

Options

Roughly in increasing order of commitment. Not mutually exclusive.

  • A — Adopt voxel, keep none. No format change, no renderer change. Much better on 4 of 5 scenes and tied-best PSNR rank among the none variants, for 21% more build time than today. Costs: the bad-snow regression is real and would ship (finding 3 — pairing it with alpha removes it), and we give up the cheapest-build position.
  • B — Adopt adaptive, keep none. Best gap rank among the none variants (5.0) and a shallower bad-snow cliff (43.9% vs 49.5%), but the most expensive option to build — 2.18× uniform and 1.79× voxel.
  • A′ — Adopt voxel and make it emit the whole pyramid in one pass (see 2a). If the pick-sequence prefix idea holds, this is strictly better than every other row: voxel's quality at roughly a third of uniform's build cost. Highest expected value on this list, and the only one whose payoff is currently unverified.
  • C — Voxel by default, uniform retained for bad-snow-like scenes. Pragmatic hybrid: take the 3.5× win everywhere it is safe and keep today's path where aggressive reduction on snow-like content regresses. Needs a decision rule, which we do not currently have a metric for at build time.
  • D — Voxel + alpha. Now the best-measuring option in the table (rank 3.2/3.2, better than none on all five scenes and cheapest to render). What blocks it is delivery, not quality: the over-unity convention lives only in ST_ALPHA_MAX/ST_PLATEAU, so no stock reader renders it correctly — and worse, the SOG writer destroys it (opacity is quantised to 8 bits of 0..1) and the LOD error pass under-reads it ~4×. See finding 7 for the fix that unblocks all three at once.
  • E — Understand wl's remaining edge on bad-cloud and bad-snow. Revised: do not port the opacity filter or the per-leaf tiling. Finding 6 shows the filter is worth under 1.2 points, and that tiling is what forces wl to depend on its coverage boost — importing it would add a problem we do not have. But wl still beats voxel on two scenes for reasons finding 6 does not explain, and those are the two scenes where voxel is weakest. Diagnosing that is the cheapest high-value experiment on this list. Note also that adopting wl wholesale costs 75% more build time than today, on CPU rather than GPU.

Method and caveats

How each cell was produced

Every splat-transform cell was regenerated for this report with the current CLI:

node bin/cli.mjs <in> <decimator flag> <target> --decimate-compensate <mode> <out>

How each level is reached differs by decimator, because the decimators differ:

  • uniform and adaptive are cascaded — the target halves at each level down to the coarsest. That is how the LOD pipeline drives them, and it is what keeps peak memory bounded (uniform peaks near 6 GB even at 2× reduction, and these sources run to 55M splats). Driver: gen.sh.
  • voxel is single-shot — its allocator reaches any target directly from the source, with no cascade of generations, so every LOD is produced from the original scene. Driver: gen-vox.sh.
  • wl is single-shot, at its shipped defaults.

So voxel and wl are strictly like-for-like, and both are compared against cascaded uniform/adaptive. That difference is a property of the algorithms, not a choice made for this report. Intermediate cascade levels were deleted as they were consumed; per-level logs are in logs/.

Caveats that affect how you read the numbers

  • wl ran single-shot, not cascaded. Its simplify goes straight to the final count in one pass, at its shipped defaults (coverage boost 0.75, opacity filter 0.05). It is an unchanged external tool so it was not re-run for this report. Boost 0.75 makes its output closest in spirit to our scale mode; it has no alpha analogue.
  • alpha output is not renderable by a stock viewer. It stores logit(D / 4), and the convention lives only in the renderer's ST_ALPHA_MAX / ST_PLATEAU environment variables — there is no CLI flag and nothing in the file records it. A reader that assumes 0..1 draws the scene roughly 4× too transparent, silently. Every alpha render here was produced with ST_ALPHA_MAX=4 ST_PLATEAU=0.40. Treat alpha as a research option pending the file-format work, not as something shippable today.
  • alpha also changes selection, not just opacity. Because alphaMax feeds the opacity decode, the first cascade level reinterprets a source written in the 0..1 convention. That shifts merge weights and selection priority, so alpha and none outputs share only 4–44% of their splat positions rather than being the same splats with different opacity. Worth deciding whether that is intended before adopting alpha.
  • ST_PLATEAU=0.40 was tuned on the uniform allocator, on a dark-only objective. It has not been re-tuned per decimator, and it is the most likely cause of any over-bright result in the alpha column.
  • Fragment counts are a cost proxy, not a frame time. The renderer breaks its per-pixel loop once transmittance saturates, so brighter splats visit fewer fragments. That makes alpha look cheap even though its drawn footprint is 4–12% larger than none.

Metric definitions

  • PSNR — 10·log10(255² / MSE), MSE over RGB, mean across 8 azimuths. "worst view" is the single worst azimuth, which is where coverage failure shows up.
  • dark>20 / bright>20 — percentage of pixels whose luma is more than 20 below / above the reference at the same camera. gap is their sum.
  • fragments ×orig — GPU-measured in-footprint fragments relative to rendering the undecimated source.
